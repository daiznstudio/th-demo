What to check before buying a Shopify store.

TrendHijacking Research Due diligenceOct 21, 20268 min read

On this page

Buying an established Shopify store can fast-track your e-commerce success. But surface metrics can hide weak traffic, thin margins or a disengaged customer list. Due diligence isn't optional. It's the job.

1. Verify the financials

Gross revenue vs. net profit

Request a verified P&L covering at least 12–24 months and examine every expense line: cost of goods, payment processing, app subscriptions and support or VA payroll.

Watch outA store showing $50,000 in monthly revenue may be spending $48,000 of it on ads and product costs.

Verify through independent dashboards

Don't rely on seller-provided CSVs or screenshots. Ask for live Shopify Admin access or a screen share, and reconcile the P&L against payouts from Shopify Payments, PayPal or Stripe.

2. Traffic sources & sustainability

Break traffic into its channels. Each carries a different risk profile:

  • Organic search: high lifetime value and the most sustainable.
  • Paid media: capital-intensive and needs constant testing.
  • Social & influencer: depends on content velocity and creator partnerships.

Check whether SEO rankings are stable or declining in Ahrefs or SEMrush, whether rising ad costs threaten ROAS, and whether social accounts and email lists are included in the sale.

Key red flagIf 90% of traffic comes from one ad account the seller is closing, you're buying a strategy you'll have to rebuild.

3. Product catalogue & suppliers

Product viability

Is this a short-lived fad or an evergreen niche? Review return and refund rates. A 20% return rate caused by quality problems can erase your margins.

Supplier due diligence

Confirm direct access to manufacturers rather than long dropshipping chains with 30-day shipping. For private-label products, request supplier agreements, MOQs and lead times, and confirm they can legally transfer to you.

4. Customer database & email health

Engagement matters far more than list size. Compare these two lists:

100,000subscribers at a 2% open rate
10,000subscribers at a 35% open rate, the better asset

Check bounce rates, spam complaints and whether automated flows exist: welcome, abandoned cart, browse abandonment and post-purchase.

5. Technical & legal health

Store audit

Review app count and site speed, whether the theme is standard or custom-coded without documentation, and the domain's history using the Wayback Machine and Google's Transparency Report.

Legal & intellectual property

Confirm ownership of the brand name, logo and designs. Check for trademark or copyright disputes and verify GDPR and CCPA compliance.

6. Operational handover

Negotiate at least 30 days of transition support (ideally 60) with a written scope covering supplier introductions, ad account structures, support training and transfer of every digital asset.

For larger deals, consider an earn-out: pay part of the price upfront and the rest over 3–6 months, tied to performance.

Final thoughts

Approach every acquisition analytically. Verify every data point, and never let a flashy revenue number make the decision for you.
